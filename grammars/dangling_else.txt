# Dangling-else grammar, Dragon Book Section 4.8.2 (grammar 4.67):
#   i = if expr then,  t = then,  e = else,  a = any other statement,  b = an expression.
# Ambiguous, so EVERY LR method has a shift-reduce conflict on 'e':
# after "i E t S" the parser cannot tell whether the 'e' belongs to this 'if'.
# Choosing SHIFT (--resolve) matches each else with the closest if, as in C.
#   try:  python lr_parser/parser.py grammars/dangling_else.txt "i b t i b t a e a" --resolve
S -> i E t S | i E t S e S | a
E -> b
